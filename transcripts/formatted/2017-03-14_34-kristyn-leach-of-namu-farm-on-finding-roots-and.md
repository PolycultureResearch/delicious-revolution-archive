But the partnership with Namu has allowed us to have those conversations in a way that people just trust or kind of look up to because Namu is such a good restaurant.

And so it just gave us a lot of opportunity to expedite the process where we weren't introducing things that were maybe somewhat familiar, but also somewhat foreign in a regular marketplace and let people's comfort with it build slowly. I know a lot of other farmers like Annabelle Lenderink, whose farm La Tercera is out of Star Route Farm and in Petaluma. She brought a lot of interesting Italian heirlooms here decades ago. And she just talks about being so committed to them because she was really rigorous in her study of Italian food and finding interesting seeds, but just that it took so much time for that to build before even something we take for granted, like lacinato kale, it had a period where it was kind of a hard sell at a market.

So just knowing the resources I had in terms of capital, the ability to be in the red as a business for how long, just knew that I did want to grow things that I believed in and I cared about, and I didn't have a ton of time to just let it sit at a market and not sell or try to constantly be pitching it to people. And with the restaurant too, they can make so many value added products and things that are just smart for any small-scale farmer to think about, in terms of converting potential waste or things that are seconds or culls at the market. I do think even if we didn't have this exclusive relationship, restaurants would comprise a pretty big part of it just because it streamlines a lot of the processes on a small farm.
